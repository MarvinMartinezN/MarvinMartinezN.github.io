Pit-Stop Robot

2.007 Design & Manufacturing I  ·  Spring 2026  ·  Individual build

A Formula-1-themed competition machine built to perform a pit stop: loosen and retighten a wheel nut, then pull a 13 lb wheel off the mount and set it back. Three subsystems, all designed and machined from raw stock inside a 12×12×16 in envelope and a 12 lb material budget.

The chassis translates fore and aft to clear the wheel. A hexagonal socket driven through a custom 3:2 acrylic gearbox handles the nut. The wheel itself is caught between two Delrin jaws pulled inward by a threaded steel rod on a chain drive. Every part was turned, milled, drilled, threaded, or laser-cut by hand — including turning round stock down to hex profile.

Envelope12 × 12 × 16 in
Mass budget12 lb
Gearbox3:2, laser-cut acrylic
ActuationLDO motors, chain drive
MaterialsAl sheet, Delrin, steel rod
CADFusion 360
Rev. A — failure analysis

Support screws added to the gearbox on the final build day over-constrained the assembly. Output torque dropped below the ~0.1 N·m needed to break the nut loose, and I hadn't re-tested after the change. The fix is procedural, not structural: tighten incrementally and check shaft freedom by hand between turns. The wheel jaws also needed better geometry — two flat blocks grip a curved tire inefficiently. Both went into the next revision.
